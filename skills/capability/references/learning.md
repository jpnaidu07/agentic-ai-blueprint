# Customer discovery and capabilities

**What:** A capability describes an outcome a user can achieve. An acceptance criterion describes observable evidence that it works. FDE discovery translates conversations and messy workflows into a bounded engineering problem.

**Why:** Before building tender chat, observe how an evaluator locates evidence and how a reviewer decides. A faster answer is useful only if it reduces a real delay without hiding evidence. Ask who owns the workflow, which systems contain truth, and what is currently measured.

**Alternatives and tradeoffs:** A process change or existing dashboard may solve the issue without custom software. Rules can handle exact eligibility conditions. RAG is appropriate only for a demonstrated document-retrieval need. Narrow scope gives earlier feedback but may omit integrations needed for adoption.

**Advanced:** Distinguish the daily user, budget sponsor and approver: their success measures may differ. Separate a leading measure (time to find a citation) from an outcome measure (review completion time); faster answers alone do not prove business value. Prioritize assumptions by impact and uncertainty.

**Practice:** Interview a proxy user using synthetic documents. Record their current steps, one bottleneck, a proposed capability, baseline measurement procedure and an acceptance example. Include a disputed rule in open_questions. Do not invent measured time savings.

**Check:** A sponsor asks for an autonomous award agent, while reviewers need evidence navigation. What gets specified first? Rubric: clarify the outcome and authority, prototype the evidence journey, preserve unresolved award policy.

**Explain:** "We reduced an ambiguous request to a testable user journey and agreed what success would mean before choosing the AI components."
